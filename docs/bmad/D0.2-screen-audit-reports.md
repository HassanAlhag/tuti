# D0.2 — Screen Audit Reports
## Design Audit Records · Customer Web · v1.0

> Each audit entry in this document is produced by applying the
> [D0.1 Design Quality Checklist](D0.1-design-quality-checklist.md)
> to a specific screen. No screen may be marked PASS without completing
> all eight steps of the D0.1 audit workflow, including the Human Taste
> Review (Section 7 of D0.1).
>
> Audits are ordered by the priority queue defined in D0.1 Section 10.
> Complete audits in priority order; do not skip ahead.

---

## How to Use This Document

1. Pick the next unaudited screen from the priority queue (lowest priority number
   with status other than PASS).
2. Open [D0.1](D0.1-design-quality-checklist.md) and work through all eight steps.
3. Copy the **Scoring Template** from D0.1 Section 9 into the matching placeholder
   section below.
4. Fill in all fields: scores, hard/soft fails, taste notes, decision, remediations.
5. Update the **Audit Index** table at the top of this document.
6. A screen may have multiple audit entries over time (early audit → remediation →
   final audit). Add new entries below the previous one; do not overwrite.

### Stage definitions

| Stage | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Early** | First-pass audit against design mockup or early implementation |
| **Final** | Audit against the shipped or near-shipped implementation; all remediations resolved |

### Status definitions

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `pending` | Not yet audited |
| `in-progress` | Audit started; scoring incomplete |
| `conditional` | Early audit passed (all ≥ 3, no hard fails); remediations outstanding |
| `pass` | Final audit passed (all ≥ 4, no hard fails, taste review done) |
| `fail` | Audit failed; cannot proceed or ship |

#### Step 7 — Human taste review

**Does this screen make me want to buy something?**
The narrative concept is right — a four-act gifting story is emotionally appropriate for a luxury marketplace. The execution makes you understand Tuti. Whether it makes you *want* to buy depends heavily on the actual photography. If the four chapter images are genuine luxury product photography (warm light, styled gift box, appetising cake), the answer is yes. If they feel like mockup renders or stock imagery, the warmth evaporates.

**Does it feel like it belongs next to a luxury perfume boutique?**
The dark forest green, white type, and editorial typographic scale feel curated. The `cubic-bezier(0.22,1,0.36,1)` easing on the copy entrance is the kind of detail a luxury brand would notice. The chapter nav (thin gold right-border, small uppercase labels) is restrained and appropriate.

**Would I be proud to show this to a high-value customer?**
Conditionally yes — the design language is correct. The two hard fails (no CTA above fold, no keyboard focus rings) are invisible to a mouse-and-eye reviewer but would be immediately apparent to a QA pass or to anyone using keyboard navigation. The missing scroll invitation on chapter 1 is the commercial concern: a high-value customer who doesn't know to scroll may leave without seeing the gift builder.

**Is there anything that feels cheap, rushed, or generic?**
The progress bar step-updates (jumping 25% per chapter rather than moving continuously) feel mechanical rather than cinematic. The chapter nav labels at 0.62rem/0.65rem are a visual edge case — they work on 1440px retina displays but feel underscaled on standard 1280px screens. The absence of any scroll invitation on chapter 1 reads as a trust assumption ("the user knows to scroll") that a luxury brand should not make.

**If I removed all the brand colours, would the layout still feel considered?**
Yes. The composition (full-bleed image, bottom-left copy, right-edge chapter nav, bottom progress line) is architecturally sound. The hierarchy holds in greyscale.

**Taste verdict:** The design intent is premium and largely achieved. Fix Batch A has addressed the safety net concern — the `.is-invite` editorial link on chapter 1 is quiet and appropriately restrained (white text, no fill, uppercase, whisper underline on hover). It does not break the narrative-first feel. The overall direction should be preserved through soft fail remediation — do not redesign.

**S7 — Deferred: `.is-copy` gap 0.85rem**

**S8 — Deferred: progress bar step-updates**

No code change. The current step-based progress (25% per chapter) correctly communicates narrative position and is appropriate for a chapter-driven layout. Deferred to a dedicated refactor story if continuous feel is prioritised.
